# G70 2024: offline weave simulation, V0 vs a delay-free error vs v1 (2026-10-04, session 6)

**Question** (from the [low-speed findings](2026-10-04-g70-lowspeed-drives.md), "Proposed next step"): would V0 with the delay removed from its error (the upstream v1 error definition), everything else unchanged, reduce the 0.3–0.6 Hz gain on this car without hurting curve tracking? Upstream v1 as-is was simulated too.

**Answer (offline model evidence; no device access):** **no.**

- In simulation, removing the delay from V0's error barely changes the 0.3–0.6 Hz gain: −0.10 to +0.03 in the controller+EPS link A.
- It adds **11–21° of lag** in the same band, and in half the cases raises the error by more than the 20% limit. It fails the criteria in every band and case, including the two where V0 itself passes.
- Upstream v1 is no better: its gain is about the same or slightly higher (−0.03 to +0.07), its lag 3° less to 13° more, and it fails the same criteria.
- **No device change is proposed for either.** The hypothesis "V0's delay-containing error drives the weave" is **not supported** by the model.

What the model points to instead, as a hypothesis:
- The steering response to torque is overdamped and lags about 90° at 0.5 Hz.
- At 35+ mph, its dynamic gain is 1.21–1.29× what latAccelFactor 4.0 assumes.
- The feedforward + P term therefore overshoots in this band, and the slow integrator can't correct it. The vehicle's yaw response adds 1.1–1.34× on top (link B, measured).

Evidence labels:
- **Confirmed source fact:** file + revision `82f58a9a8c` (WSL clone, clean).
- **Observation:** from rlogs of routes 6, 7, 8, 0a, 0b.
- **Model result:** simulation output; valid only as far as the plant model is (see "Model adequacy").
- **Hypothesis.**

## Method

Scripts are in `E:\DragalPilot\scratch\wsl\s5\`, outputs in `E:\DragalPilot\analysis\g70-weave-sim-20261004\`, and logs in `E:\DragalPilot\logs\s5-*.log`.

Environment:
- The checkout venv: Python 3.12.13, numpy 2.5.2, for the source-class runs.
- `~/venvs/vid`: Python 3.12.13, numpy 2.5.3, scipy 1.18.1. Made by the video session; used read-only.

1. **Dataset** (`s5_dataset.py`): one row per controlsd cycle, on the controlsState time base.
   - Latest carState, vehicleParameters and lateralDelay at that time.
   - The carOutput torque.
   - Kalman yaw rate.
2. **The controllers are the checkout's own code** (`s5_ctrl.py`): `latcontrol_torque_v0.py` and upstream `latcontrol_torque.py`, imported on x86 with the sunnypilot extension replaced by a no-op stub.
   - The extension is inactive on this car: NNLC 0, LateralJerkTorqueController 0, TorqueParamsOverrideEnabled 0, EnforceTorqueControl absent.
   - **v0e** is V0's file with exactly one line changed: `setpoint = lat_delay * desired_lateral_jerk + expected_lateral_accel` → `setpoint = expected_lateral_accel`.
   - The Hyundai output stage is `carcontroller.py` L87–99 plus `apply_driver_steer_torque_limits`.
   - A scalar re-implementation (`s5_fast.py`) is used for speed.
3. **Harness checks** (observation):
   - **Source V0 replay on logged inputs** (`s5_replay_check.py`, 5 routes):
     - setpoint exact (≤ 2.4e-7);
     - measurement p99 ≤ 0.004 m/s²;
     - P/F/output p99 ≤ 0.007;
     - integrator p99 ≤ 0.003, except route 8 at 0.027;
     - carOutput reproduced exactly on 81–95% of cycles, max 0.023.
   - **Fast vs source on identical inputs** (`s5_equiv_check.py`, routes 0b and 7 complete, all three variants): torque ≤ 6.2e-8, output identical.
   - **Fast open-loop replay vs logs** (`s5_replay_detail.py`): output within 1 count of STEER_MAX on 99.1–99.9% of cycles.
   - **Timing** (observation, `s5_outstage_check.py`, 100% exact match):
     - the command of cycle k is the carOutput of cycle k+1, limited with the driver torque of carState k+1;
     - so controlsd's `steer_limited_by_safety` compares with the output from two cycles back.
     - The simulation uses this timing. An earlier simulation batch lacked the extra cycle; it was rerun and its output renamed `*_superseded_timing`.
4. **Plant identification** (`s5_plant_id.py`):
   - **Input:** carOutput torque × 4.0 (sign per the controllers' convention).
   - **Output:** the controllers' own measurement (`actualLateralAccel`, from steering angle).
   - **Model:** per speed band, a 2nd-order torque-driven steering model with Coulomb friction (tanh), transport delay, a roll term and a bias.
   - **Fit:** output-error (simulated), on 20 s chunks of clean engaged runs (no press, no blinker within 3 s) of **routes 8, 0a and 0b**.
   - **Held out:** **routes 6 and 7** (old tune, latAccelFactor 2.7, friction 0.11).
5. **Closed-loop simulation** (`s5_sim.py`):
   - **Loop:** logged request (`desiredCurvature · v²`), lat delay, speed, roll and driver torque → controller → output stage → plant.
   - **Runs:** clean engaged runs ≥ 30 s.
   - **Two modes:**
     - **clean:** only the requests drive the loop;
     - **dist:** the plant model's output residual on the logged run is re-injected. With V0 this reproduces the log to an RMS of 0.004–0.016 m/s².
   - **Links:** A = request → measurement. C = A × B_emp, where B_emp = measurement → yaw lateral accel, measured from the same runs (not simulated). Same H1/Welch method as `s4b_loop_xfer.py`.
   - **Curve ratio** and **error** use `lateral_metrics.py` definitions. The error is reported against the delayed request (the v1 definition) and against the current request.
6. **Synthetic tests** (`s5_step.py`): request steps (0.3 and 1.0 m/s², ISO jerk) and sines (0.15–0.6 Hz, 0.2 and 0.5 m/s²) per band, no disturbance.
7. **Exploratory diagnostic, not a candidate** (`v0k`): V0 with latAccelFactor scaled by the fitted plant gain K of each band. It asks one question: does matching the feedforward to the plant's dynamic gain matter? K comes from the fit routes, so on those routes it is not held-out evidence.

## Interpretation (hypothesis)

- The ~3 s weave is consistent with a closed loop that amplifies 0.3–0.6 Hz content at 35+ mph. The main reasons are:
  - the plant's dynamic torque gain is above the controller's assumption;
  - the plant has substantial lag;
  - the car's yaw response overshoots on top.
- **The delay term in V0's error is not the driver.** Neither v0e nor v1 would help.
- The model **does not explain the low-speed impression.** At 20–35 mph it predicts A < 1, while the logs show 1.19 at 0.4–0.6 Hz. So the low-speed weave is either outside this plant model (driver hands, friction behaviour, outer loop through the model's re-planning) or comes from the request itself.

## Limitations

- **Single-input plant.** Driver hand torque, road disturbances and the vehicle-to-steering coupling are all lumped into the residual. The residual in 0.2–0.6 Hz is 0.04–0.05 m/s², which is 35–72% of the band motion.
- **Parameters are constant within each speed band.**
- **20–35 mph** has only 227 s of usable runs and no held-out route.
- **The outer loop isn't simulated:** the model re-planning against the car's motion. The logged requests are replayed unchanged.
- **C = A × B_emp is an approximation.** On the logs, A×B underestimates C at 0.4–0.6 Hz above 55 mph by about 0.2 (1.53 vs 1.72).
- **Absolute gains carry about ±0.15 and lags about ±13° of model error** (see the adequacy table).
- **v0k is exploratory.** Its K comes from the fit routes, and it is not a proposal.
- **This is offline model evidence only.** It says nothing about hardware or the car's physical response beyond the logs it was fitted to.

## Options (user decision)

1. **No device change from this round.** v0e and v1 are not indicated (criteria failed; worse lag).
2. If the weave is worth pursuing further, the model points to a **car-scoped feedforward scale by speed** (latAccelFactor depends on speed), as a new hypothesis. It would need its own offline validation on new held-out drives before any proposal:
   - steps and curves;
   - interaction with torqued, which applies one learned latAccelFactor once valid (~4.2).
   - The C ≤ 1.2 criterion should be revisited, because the vehicle link alone exceeds it.
3. **Better data/model first:**
   - more engaged 20–35 mph and stop-and-go driving, where the model is weakest and the user feels the weave most;
   - a plant model with driver torque as an input.
